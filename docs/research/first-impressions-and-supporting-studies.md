# First impressions and supporting studies

This document preserves the wider research discussed before the focused personality/video review. These studies help define features, outcomes, and comparisons. Their contribution is labeled by the outcome actually studied: initial appeal, selection, conversational connection, measurement, or computational benchmarking.

For the detailed zero-acquaintance cue results, see [Back 2011](back-2011-cue-analysis.md). For photograph-based actual-personality accuracy, see [Naumann and related studies](visual-cues-and-personality.md). Direct pairing/enjoyment evidence appears in [personality and conversation outcomes](personality-and-conversation-outcomes.md).

## User personality and appearance preferences


### Lönnqvist, Ilmarinen & Verkasalo (2021)

*Who likes whom? The interaction between perceiver personality and target look.* Journal of Research in Personality. [Full paper](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/84952839-37a7-4614-ac34-0c61c15875b5/content)

**385 undergraduate observers**, 87.5% women, mean age 25.4, rated photographs of 146 strangers, generating 5,039 ratings. Higher openness predicted more favorable responses to unconventional/distinctive appearances; openness × conventional appearance **b=−.05, p=.001**, and × ordinary appearance **b=−.04, p=.005**. Other reported interactions concerned agreeableness, extraversion, conscientiousness, neuroticism, and specific appearance factors.

Supports testing **user personality × visible presentation**, not target-personality diagnosis or actual enjoyment. See Table 3; additional qualitative associations are preserved below. A non-Duchenne smile describes a movement pattern, not dishonesty.

### Selfhout and colleagues (2010)

*Emerging Late Adolescent Friendship Networks and Big Five Personality Traits: A Social Network Approach.* [Full paper](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/1874/384875/1/Selfhout_et_al_2010_Journal_of_Personality.pdf)

**205 students**, about age 19, five measurement occasions during the first university year. Network models associated extraversion with selecting more friends, agreeableness with receiving nominations, and similarity in openness/extraversion/agreeableness with selection. Reported similarity parameters were 1.45/1.46/1.55, respectively, p<.001. These are model-specific network parameters, not correlations or success probabilities. Selection is distinct from satisfaction. Table 2, printed p.525.

### De La Mare & Lee (2023)

*Assortative Preferences for Personality and Online Dating Apps.* [Full paper](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/retrieve/de57b6ca-6bdc-448d-8d30-40684ed9bd0c/1-s2.0-S0191886923001083-main.pdf)

**383 heterosexual participants**, mean age 25.4, evaluated 100 simulated dating profiles each, combining faces and written personality descriptions. Similarity-related choice interactions: agreeableness **b=.06**, extraversion **.07**, openness **.07**; comparable conscientiousness/emotional-stability interactions were nonsignificant. These are romantic match choices based partly on explicit written information, not observed enjoyment or traits inferred from video. Table 1 and Results 3.2–3.3.

### de Matos Fernandes and colleagues (2025; online 2024)

*How Do Personality Traits Affect the Formation of Friendship and Preference-for-Collaboration Networks?* [Full paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/02654075241297721)

**95 Dutch first-year students**, 61% women, mean age 19.5. Openness predicted friend selection **estimate .38, SE .11**, and collaborator selection **.35, SE .09**. Similarity effects were nonsignificant. Small sample and low openness reliability, **α=.26**, constrain interpretation. Measured collaborator preferences, not successful collaboration. Supports separating social enjoyment from professional usefulness. Tables 2 and 4.

### Montoya, Horton & Kirchner (2008)

*Is Actual Similarity Necessary for Attraction? A Meta-Analysis of Actual and Perceived Similarity.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407508096700)

Reported **460 effect sizes from 313 investigations**: actual similarity–attraction **r=.47**, perceived similarity–attraction **r=.39**. Moderation by relationship stage matters. The pooled results include attitudes and other similarities; they are **not** effect sizes for Big Five matching from video. Perceived similarity can also be influenced by liking.

## Conversation quality studies

These help define outcomes and during-conversation features. They do not replace the personality-pairing question.

| Paper | Design/findings discussed | Scope and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Sprecher, online 2021 / issue 2023, expectations in getting acquainted](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-021-01466-4) | 142 students/71 pairs; 15-minute Skype conversation. Expected liking correlated with later enjoyment **r=.20**; expected enjoyment **r=.41**. Similarity information increased expectations without a significant enjoyment increase. | Supports collecting the user's own expectation; does not establish an appearance-only prediction or causality of expectations. |
| [Sprecher et al. (2013), *Taking turns: Reciprocal self-disclosure promotes liking in initial interactions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.017) | 156 undergraduates; alternating disclosure produced greater enjoyment, liking, closeness, and perceived similarity than separated disclosure/listening roles. | Numerical effect size was not verified from accessible material. No universal ideal speaking-time ratio established. |
| [Templeton et al. (2022), *Fast response times signal social connection in conversation*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2116915119) | 66 undergraduates, 322 ten-minute conversations in Study 1. Faster partner responses associated with enjoyment, **b=−.27, SE=.05**. Manipulated recordings changed observers' judgments. | Timing needs audio. Observer experiments do not show that forcing faster responses makes participants enjoy a conversation. |
| [Huang et al. (2017), *It Doesn't Hurt to Ask: Question-Asking Increases Liking*](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Huang%20et%20al%202017_6945bc5e-3b3e-4c0a-addd-254c9e603c60.pdf) | Study 1: 398 participants/199 text-chat pairs. High question-askers were liked more: 5.79 vs 5.31, **d=.35**. | Primarily liking; follow-up-question associations and question-quantity manipulations are not identical causal tests. Requires content. |
| [West et al. (2025), *High-quality listening behaviors linked to social connection between strangers*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2) | Secondary analyses: 300 and 348 participants. Study 2 overall listening **β=.21**, validation **β=.26** for partner connection. Follow-up frequency did not predict that outcome in the small-talk setting. | Connection measure was positivity resonance. Behavior combinations and context matter; coded in 30-second intervals. |
| [Kardas, Kumar & Epley (2022), *Overly Shallow?*](https://michaelkardas.com/assets/pdfs/Kardas%2C%20Kumar%2C%20%26%20Epley%20%282022%29%20Overly%20shallow%20Miscalibrated%20expectations%20create%20a%20barrier%20to%20deeper%20conversation.pdf) | Experiment 6b: 120 participants experienced shallow and deeper conversations. Subsequent preference favored deeper conversation, **d=.27, p=.004**. Connection improved; happiness difference was nonsignificant. | Not every positive outcome changes together. Not a rationale to impose unwanted personal questions. |

## Reviews, books, cue-specific studies, and datasets

These sources were also discussed and are preserved in the historical source notes. Most are context or methods, not personality-pair satisfaction evidence.

| Source | Contribution and restriction |
|---|---|
| [Hall, Horgan & Murphy (2019), *Nonverbal Communication*](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-103145) | Review of face, voice, body, and interpersonal space; vocabulary and context, not a scoring formula. |
| [Funder (1995), *On the Accuracy of Personality Judgment: A Realistic Approach*](https://rap.ucr.edu/FunderPR1995.pdf) | Relevant cues must be available, detected, and used appropriately. Framework, not validation of event enjoyment predictions. |

| [Walsh & Hewitt (1985), *Giving Men the Come-On*](https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1985.61.3.873) | Directed eye contact plus smiling affected approaches in a narrow bar setting; approach, not satisfaction. |

| [Raman et al. (2022), ConfLab](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/95f9ad2e251e9014697589037450f9bb-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) | Professional networking dataset, 48 participants; useful for behavior/group annotation, not personalized satisfaction labels. |
| [Jacques Junior et al. (2019), vision-based apparent personality survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08046) | Apparent-trait prediction methods and pitfalls; observer labels are not actual personality or enjoyment. |
| [Montoya, Kershaw & Prosser (2018), attraction and enacted behavior meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000148) | 309 effects, N=5,422; attraction–behavior association, not pre-approach individualized enjoyment validation. |
| [Soto & John (2017), BFI-2 materials paper used in discussion](https://www.colby.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Soto_John_2017b.pdf) | Measurement foundation. Link covers abbreviated forms; distinguish it from the full-inventory development paper when selecting citations/scoring instructions. |
| [Hall, Schmid Mast & West, eds. (2016), *The Social Psychology of Perceiving Others Accurately*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781316181959) | Academic book on interpersonal accuracy; cite specific chapters for empirical claims. |
| [Kendon (1990), *Conducting Interaction*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Conducting_Interaction.html?id=7-8zAAAAIAAJ) | Gaze, greetings, attention, spatial organization; useful operational vocabulary. |
| [Bakeman & Quera (2011), *Sequential Analysis and Observational Methods for the Behavioral Sciences*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139017343) | Coding design, rater training, reliability, and sequences. |
| [Gosling (2008), *Snoop*](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/sam-gosling/snoop/9780465013821/) | Accessible research book about environments/possessions; accumulated environments differ from one incidental event object. |

Other leads include Fischer & Ram's 2024 conversational-dynamics preprint ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11043v1)) and a stranger-interaction social-cognition study ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10516339/)); neither is adopted here as verified personality-pairing evidence. The Hayashi rapport-estimation preprint has since been inspected and is now covered in [personality and conversation outcomes](personality-and-conversation-outcomes.md).

## Additional Lönnqvist findings retained from the discussion

The study also reported associations involving extraversion and prepared/traditional/attractive appearances, agreeableness and distinctive/unconventional appearances, conscientiousness and energetic appearances or non-Duchenne smiles, and neuroticism and non-Duchenne smiles. Earlier notes recorded single-interaction estimates for extraversion × traditional appearance b=.03, p=.032; agreeableness × traditional appearance b=−.03, p=.021; conscientiousness × energetic appearance b=.03, p=.029 (Table 3). These are small, model-specific initial-appeal associations. The two openness estimates above remain the primary numerical examples. A non-Duchenne smile refers to a movement pattern, not dishonesty; neither low trait scores nor absence of a cue guarantees the reverse preference. [Author paper](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/84952839-37a7-4614-ac34-0c61c15875b5/content).

| Source | Earlier discussion and use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Ingram & Morris (2007), Do People Mix at Mixers?](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/do-people-mix-mixers-structure-homophily-and-life-party) | Business-mixer observations discussed the influence of existing acquaintances on encounters. | Encounter structure does not establish enjoyment with an unfamiliar candidate. |
| [Rohrer, Keller & Elwert (2021), Proximity can induce diverse friendships](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0255097) | Earlier notes: randomized seating, N=2,966 students, mutual friendship 15% versus 22% for adjacent seating. | School proximity/friendship outcome, not adult pre-approach compatibility. |
| [Epley & Schroeder (2014), Mistakenly seeking solitude](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25019381/) | Strangers' interest and the experience of connecting can be underestimated. | Supports measuring expectations and exploration, not predicting a specific pair from video. |

| [Kong et al. (2026), SIV-Bench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1863/) | Social-scene description, social-state reasoning, and prediction need separate evaluation. | Earlier technical lead; not a validated AVocadoEyes predictor. |
| [Matz et al. | [Vinciarelli, Pantic & Bourlard (2009), Social signal processing](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~vincia/papers/sspsurvey.pdf) | Computational vocabulary connecting behavior and social interpretation. | A field survey, not personalized satisfaction validation. |
| [Goffman (1963), Behavior in Public Places](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Behavior-in-Public-Places/Erving-Goffman/9780029119402) | Gathering boundaries and public encounters. | Conceptual foundation, not quantitative predictor. |
